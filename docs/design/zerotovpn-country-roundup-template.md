# ZeroToVPN visual country-roundup template

Status: preferred commercial country-guide direction

Visual reference: `zerotovpn-country-roundup-concept-v1-iran.png`

First target: `/blog/best-vpn-for-iran-2026-bypass-internet-censorship`

Evidence companion: `/countries/iran`

The concept demonstrates the reusable layout, not a new source of facts. Provider rankings, prices, test labels, feature support, dates, and availability must come from the verified project data layer. A concept label such as “ZeroToVPN checked” may only ship when the corresponding evidence record exists.

## Page objective

Answer four questions in a single, scan-friendly journey:

1. What is the cautious short answer for this country and network context?
2. Which providers are worth evaluating, and what is each provider's main limitation?
3. What evidence supports the comparison, and what remains unverified?
4. What should the reader prepare, compare, or read next?

This is a commercial editorial pillar. The country route remains the evidence and safety companion. Both pages link to each other prominently; they are not near-duplicate doorway pages.

## Visual direction

- Continue the deep-navy, electric-blue, cyan, lime, warm-off-white system used by the homepage and review template.
- Use an editorial serif for the H1 and major section headings; use a clear sans-serif for navigation, tables, captions, and controls.
- Target 55–60% visual surface above the fold.
- Place one meaningful image, screenshot, chart, table, or evidence module every 300–450 words.
- Alternate media and copy in provider deep dives so the page feels like a magazine feature, not a repeating card grid.
- Use country imagery calmly and contextually. Avoid political dramatization, hacker clichés, anonymous hooded figures, and fear-led art direction.

## Page anatomy

### 1. Editorial header

- research announcement bar;
- compact site header;
- breadcrumbs;
- `COUNTRY GUIDE` eyebrow;
- exactly one descriptive H1;
- answer-led dek;
- author, reviewer, real update date, and evidence-check date;
- visible affiliate disclosure before the first commercial link;
- original country/network visual.

For Iran, keep the existing cautious title and evidence boundary. Do not promise that any VPN works on every Iranian network, device, place, or date.

### 2. Direct-answer layer

Use two connected panels:

- a dark `QUICK ANSWER` panel with the most important limitation and three preparation checks;
- one `Evaluate first` provider panel with approved product imagery, a bounded verdict, three supported strengths, one honest limitation, an internal review link, and one tracked `Check current price` CTA.

Do not show a price, refund term, discount, or feature count unless it is fresh and verified. Provider imagery must be stripped of source-site navigation and campaign copy.

### 3. Context criteria

Show four compact visual criteria that explain the ranking logic. The Iran implementation uses:

- obfuscation or stealth documentation;
- official app access and update path;
- reconnect and failure behavior;
- legal and personal-safety boundary.

Every criterion can carry one of three evidence states:

- `Direct test`;
- `Provider-reported`;
- `Not yet verified` or `Verify locally`.

Never collapse those states into a single generic checkmark.

### 4. Anchor navigation

Use a sticky horizontal row for:

- Quick answer;
- Compare;
- Top picks;
- How we test;
- Prepare;
- Safety;
- FAQ.

On mobile, make this horizontally scrollable. Do not introduce a persistent affiliate overlay.

### 5. Top-three overview

Each ranked provider card contains:

- provider logo and approved app or device image;
- contextual label such as `Evaluate first`, `Alternative`, or `Stealth option`;
- short verdict;
- one advantage;
- one drawback;
- internal `Read review` link;
- one tracked `Check current price` CTA.

Do not use a country-specific score or star rating without a documented country-specific scoring basis. Commercial relationships do not determine the order.

### 6. Provider comparison

Use a semantic HTML table with a caption, `thead`, `tbody`, and row headers. Recommended columns:

- VPN;
- documented obfuscation or stealth mode;
- mobile platforms and official distribution path;
- evidence status and last verification date;
- most important limitation;
- full review;
- current verified price.

The provider name and current-price label may be natural affiliate links. Do not make every cell clickable. Keep the provider column sticky on narrow screens and offer a stacked top-three fallback.

### 7. Provider deep dives

Use one reusable component with alternating image placement. Each entry includes:

- numbered provider heading;
- ideal user and concise verdict;
- approved screenshot or editorial product image;
- why it made the shortlist;
- a test-note chart or evidence panel;
- pros and cons;
- explicit limitation;
- fresh pricing context when verified;
- one natural inline affiliate link;
- internal full-review link;
- at most one primary CTA.

The first Iran entry should explain NordVPN's documented obfuscated-server requirements without turning documentation into proof of current connectivity from Iran.

### 8. Testing and evidence

Use a dark visual block with four stages:

1. provider documentation;
2. official app access;
3. restricted-network simulation or direct network test;
4. failure and reconnect behavior.

Show device, OS, app version, protocol, date, network context, server region, and failure conditions wherever a result is presented. Clearly separate direct in-country evidence from simulation and provider claims. Link to the full methodology and saved evidence dossier.

### 9. Preparation sequence

Use four illustrated steps:

1. install official apps before the restrictive network is encountered;
2. update, sign in, and confirm recovery and support access;
3. test more than one provider-documented protocol;
4. retain a lawful fallback and stop when the legal or operational boundary is unclear.

This section is preparation and risk framing, not advice for hacking, piracy, criminal conduct, or evading an organisation's security controls.

### 10. Safety boundary

Place a high-visibility caution panel before the FAQ:

- availability and rules can change;
- another person's success is not proof for the reader's network;
- a VPN does not authorise unlawful activity;
- this page is not legal or personal-safety advice;
- link to the country evidence checklist and current authoritative sources.

### 11. FAQ and cluster continuation

Use visible answers for five to eight genuine PAA/search-intent questions. Add FAQ schema only for the questions rendered on the page.

End with three image-led contextual cluster cards, followed by descriptive inline links where appropriate:

- Iran evidence checklist;
- VPN obfuscation explained;
- VPN protocol comparison;
- VPNs for messaging;
- Android and iPhone guides;
- China and Russia evidence pages;
- provider reviews;
- methodology.

Avoid a generic link dump and avoid repeating the primary keyword unnaturally in anchors.

### 12. Newsletter and footer

The newsletter form can also be offered in an email-collection exit-intent modal. It cannot contain an affiliate offer, NordVPN discount, coupon, countdown, cashback, reward, or provider CTA.

## Affiliate and NordVPN guardrails

- Disclosure appears before the first affiliate link.
- Route commercial links through the existing attribution layer and deterministic sub-ID helper.
- Use `rel="sponsored nofollow noopener noreferrer"` where the current component layer requires it.
- Use approved NordVPN assets only on relevant VPN/privacy content eligible under the program rules.
- No unsolicited email promotion, pop-under advertising, comment spam, black-hat SEO, hidden text, doorway pages, unassigned coupons, incentives, or trademark domains.
- Do not place NordVPN affiliate links next to content that promotes hacking, piracy, unlawful file sharing, or other criminal conduct.
- Do not bid on NordVPN branded search terms.
- No fake scarcity, invented savings, or access guarantee.

## Iran migration notes

The current commercial pillar already has strong fundamentals: short answer, evidence boundary, three candidates, FAQ/PAA, sources, author trust, internal cluster links, canonical/hreflang metadata, and tracked quick-pick CTAs. Preserve those signals while changing composition.

Fix alongside implementation:

- use an Iran-specific social image instead of the generic OG image;
- gate the English editorial override to English, so translated hreflang URLs do not silently render the English title and body;
- turn the existing evaluation-criteria table into a provider comparison;
- add inline visual evidence to the long-form article;
- keep `/countries/iran` non-commercial and strengthen the two-way link between pillar and evidence companion.

## Acceptance criteria

- The cautious answer, evidence state, disclosure, date, first shortlist, and next action are visible above the fold.
- Exactly one H1 and logical heading nesting.
- No production fact comes from the concept image.
- Every provider claim has a source type and check date.
- Every table is accessible and every chart has a text equivalent.
- The primary comparison is useful without clicking an affiliate link.
- Images are responsive, correctly sized, and do not cause layout shift.
- Mobile users can reach every section without a blocking commercial overlay.
- Newsletter exit intent contains no affiliate promotion.
- Affiliate attribution, localization, editorial-audit, and structured-data tests remain green.
