# ZeroToVPN visual head-to-head comparison template

Status: preferred two-provider comparison direction

Visual reference: `zerotovpn-head-to-head-concept-v1-nordvpn-vs-surfshark.png`

First target: `/compare/nordvpn-vs-surfshark`

The concept establishes layout and decision flow. It is not a source for prices, ratings, audit dates, speed results, feature support, or provider claims. All production values must come from one verified comparison model with claim-level dates and evidence states.

## Page objective

Help the reader move through three layers:

1. understand the meaningful difference in roughly 30 seconds;
2. inspect comparable evidence for the categories that matter;
3. choose a provider, an alternative, or neither without being pushed by a false universal winner.

The governing principle is: explain the difference, prove it, then convert.

## Visual direction

- Continue the homepage, review, and country-roundup system: deep navy, electric blue, cyan, lime, warm off-white, and charcoal.
- Use provider colors only inside the relevant provider surface. NordVPN receives pale blue; Surfshark receives teal and charcoal.
- Give both providers equal visual weight, component depth, and CTA prominence.
- Target 55–60% product imagery above the fold.
- Prefer real app screens, test setups, result charts, and matched device tasks over decorative stock images.
- Use an editorial serif for the H1 and major section headings; use a clean sans-serif for tables, captions, navigation, and controls.
- Do not use boxing gloves, fighting graphics, childish `VS` explosions, or winner confetti.

## Page anatomy

### 1. Editorial comparison header

- research announcement bar;
- compact site header;
- breadcrumbs;
- `HEAD-TO-HEAD` eyebrow;
- exactly one H1;
- concise trade-off dek;
- author, reviewer, real update date, and real last-tested date;
- methodology link;
- visible affiliate disclosure before the first commercial link.

Do not print the current month as a cosmetic freshness signal. Display the date attached to the underlying review, price, or test evidence.

### 2. Split comparison hero

Use equal provider halves and a centered editorial verdict:

- provider logo;
- approved product or app visual;
- no more than three important differentiators;
- equal `Check current price` CTAs;
- `Best overall`, `Best for`, and `Closest call` statements only when supported;
- a reminder that the best choice depends on use case.

Do not show unverified prices, discounts, coupons, review counts, or category scores here.

### 3. Thirty-second answer

Use three parallel decision cards:

- `Choose [provider A] if…`;
- `Choose [provider B] if…`;
- `Choose neither if…`.

Each card contains three short reasons tied to later evidence. The `neither` path is essential editorially and commercially: it makes room for privacy-first, open-source, free-tier, or simpler alternatives.

### 4. Anchor navigation

Use a sticky horizontal row for:

- Verdict;
- Test results;
- Privacy;
- Streaming;
- Apps;
- Pricing;
- Alternatives;
- FAQ.

On mobile, make it horizontally scrollable. Do not use a sticky affiliate banner or persistent commercial overlay.

### 5. Category-winner matrix

Recommended rows:

- download speed;
- latency and stability;
- privacy evidence;
- security tools;
- streaming reliability;
- restricted-network support;
- app usability;
- device allowance;
- introductory value;
- renewal transparency.

Every row contains:

- winner or `Tie`;
- a one-sentence reason;
- evidence state;
- a link to the detailed section or artifact.

Supported evidence states:

- `Lab tested`;
- `Desk verified`;
- `Provider stated`;
- `Needs retest`.

A tie is a valid result. Do not manufacture a winner from a trivial numerical difference or an opaque aggregate score.

### 6. Comparable test story

Show only tests run under equivalent conditions:

- same devices and operating systems;
- same protocols where possible;
- same locations and time windows;
- same sample sizes and aggregation method;
- median and variation, not only the best run;
- test date and baseline.

Recommended visual modules:

- grouped speed-retention chart;
- latency and stability plot;
- four compact metrics;
- test-setup image;
- evidence drawer and methodology link.

Every chart needs a text equivalent. Do not mix provider claims with first-party measurements without labelling the source.

### 7. Privacy and security

Give both providers an equal-width evidence card with:

- jurisdiction;
- logging-policy evidence;
- audit timeline and scope;
- kill-switch test;
- DNS, WebRTC, and IPv6 leak checks;
- relevant protocol or security features;
- one explicit limitation;
- source type and last verification date.

Explain shared ownership where relevant without implying shared infrastructure, policies, or evidence.

### 8. Matched app experience

Compare the same tasks on both services:

- onboarding;
- connect;
- change server;
- find and activate the kill switch;
- reach specialty features;
- use mobile, desktop, and TV apps.

Use matched phone and laptop frames plus step count, friction note, and platform/date. This section evaluates usability; it is not a marketing-feature collage.

### 9. Streaming and everyday-use scenes

Use three to four image-led use cases such as:

- streaming;
- travel and public Wi-Fi;
- families and many devices;
- restrictive networks.

Each card names a contextual winner or tie, gives one reason, and links to the evidence. Do not turn platform access into a permanent guarantee.

### 10. Pricing and renewal truth table

Use equal provider columns and show:

- effective introductory monthly equivalent;
- required total upfront payment;
- term and included months;
- renewal price and renewal term;
- monthly plan;
- refund period and eligibility boundary;
- device allowance;
- currency and tax caveat;
- price last checked.

Natural price phrases can be tracked affiliate links. Each provider receives one equal CTA. Use `Check current price`, not `exclusive deal`, fake savings, or a countdown. When a value is stale or unavailable, say `Verify at checkout` rather than guessing.

### 11. Equal-depth provider chapters

Use two mirrored editorial sections:

- where the provider pulls ahead;
- where it falls short;
- who benefits from that difference;
- visual evidence;
- full-review internal link;
- one contextual affiliate CTA.

The second provider must not become an abbreviated loser card.

### 12. Pick by use case

Use compact decision cards for:

- streaming;
- frequent travel;
- many devices;
- beginners;
- advanced controls;
- long-term value.

Each outcome states why and links to its evidence. `Tie` and `Neither` remain available outcomes.

### 13. Alternatives

Show three editorial alternatives linked to a specific unmet need, for example:

- Proton VPN for a credible free tier and open-source apps;
- Mullvad for minimal account collection;
- ExpressVPN for a simpler premium experience.

Use verified current descriptions and internal review links. Affiliate CTAs are optional here; the primary comparison should remain the focus.

### 14. Methodology and accountability

Use a full-width dark evidence block showing:

- devices;
- protocols;
- locations;
- testing window;
- category weights when a total score exists;
- evidence legend;
- unverified items;
- methodology link;
- change log.

If the evidence cannot support an overall score, publish category outcomes without one.

### 15. FAQ, related comparisons, and newsletter

- Build visible FAQ answers from current DataForSEO/PAA evidence and add schema only for those answers.
- Link naturally to both full reviews, the best-VPN pillar, privacy and streaming guides, protocols, relevant country pages, methodology, and a small set of genuine comparisons.
- Keep reverse provider-pair URLs redirected or canonicalized to one deterministic order.
- The email exit-intent form may collect newsletter subscribers, but it contains no affiliate offer, coupon, discount, provider CTA, or countdown.

## Affiliate guardrails

- Disclosure precedes the first affiliate link.
- Use the existing affiliate components, click beacon, Short.io handling, and deterministic NordVPN `aff_sub` attribution.
- Commercial links use the project's sponsored/nofollow policy.
- Provider names are not automatically affiliate links everywhere.
- Natural inline links are appropriate when the sentence is specifically about current price, a plan, checkout, or subscribing.
- No unsolicited email promotion, pop-under advertising, unassigned coupons, incentives, hidden text, doorway pages, fake urgency, or unsupported savings claims.
- Give both providers equal commercial opportunity; ranking follows evidence, not commission.

## NordVPN versus Surfshark migration notes

The current route already provides canonical metadata, hreflang URLs, breadcrumb and FAQ schema, daily revalidation, affiliate tracking, a feature table, pros/cons, and related links. Preserve the reliable infrastructure, but do not preserve unsupported claims or cosmetic freshness.

Fix during implementation:

- move disclosure above the first affiliate link;
- replace the generic OG image with a page-specific comparison image;
- normalize `/compare/surfshark-vs-nordvpn` to `/compare/nordvpn-vs-surfshark` using a redirect or canonical ordering;
- localize the actual body, not metadata alone;
- consolidate comparison and review values into one verified model;
- resolve conflicting prices, server counts, ratings, and protocol lists before rendering;
- replace auto-generated `in our tests` FAQ claims with evidence-backed answers;
- replace the current-month update label with real evidence dates;
- avoid putting stale price data into `Offer` schema;
- expose author, reviewer, methodology, sources, and test artifacts;
- add reciprocal links from both reviews and relevant use-case pillars.

## Acceptance criteria

- Difference, trade-off, disclosure, evidence date, and both next actions are visible above the fold.
- Exactly one H1 and logical heading nesting.
- Provider visual weight and CTA prominence are equivalent.
- Each category outcome has a reason, source type, and verification date.
- Ties and `Neither` outcomes render correctly.
- All charts have text equivalents and all tables have captions and accessible headers.
- Introductory and renewal pricing are distinct and verified at render time.
- No production claim is sourced from the concept image.
- Only one canonical URL exists for a provider pair.
- Localized routes contain genuinely localized visible content.
- Mobile users can reach every section without a blocking commercial overlay.
- Affiliate attribution, localization, structured-data, editorial-audit, and accessibility tests remain green.
